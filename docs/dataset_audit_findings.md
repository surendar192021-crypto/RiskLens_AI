# Dataset audit: findings and decisions (Phase 2)

This file interprets the measured results in [`dataset_audit.md`](dataset_audit.md), which is
generated by `python -m scripts.run_audit`. Numbers here are copied from that run (27 Oct 2026).
Labels used: **Observed** = measured in the data · **Decision** = project choice ·
**Limitation** = what the data cannot support.

## 1. Summary

- **Observed:** all 10 files are present and readable: 9 data tables (about 58.5 million rows in total)
  plus the column-description file. Every table's primary key is unique and has no nulls.
- **Observed:** `application_train` has 307,511 applicants, and 24,825 of them (8.07%) have
  `TARGET = 1`, so there are 11.39 negatives per positive.
- **Observed:** every date is "days or months relative to the current application". No history
  record falls after the application date, apart from 17 data errors (section 4).
- **Limitation:** there are no absolute dates and no follow-up of the current loan. Confirmed
  consequences: no temporal split, no survival analysis on `TARGET`, and no post-origination monitoring.
- **New option:** the side tables contain *observed monthly histories of earlier credits*. This
  supports two scoped alternatives: a delinquency-migration matrix and a survival study on
  previous loans (section 8).

## 2. Target

**Observed** official definition: *"1 - client with payment difficulties: he/she had late payment
more than X days on at least one of the first Y installments of the loan in our sample, 0 - all other cases."*

- **Limitation:** X and Y are not disclosed. `TARGET` is an early-repayment-difficulty flag. It is
  not a legal default, and it does not cover the full life of the loan. The report and dashboard
  will say "payment difficulty" or "default risk (as defined by TARGET)", never "will default".
- **Limitation:** "in our sample": the training data is a sample chosen by Home Credit, and its
  8.07% rate need not match any real portfolio's default rate. Calibrated probabilities are
  calibrated *to this sample*.
- **Decision:** with an 11:1 class imbalance, model selection will use PR-AUC and ROC-AUC, plus
  the Brier score for probability quality. Accuracy is not used, because predicting "no
  difficulty" for everyone already scores 91.9%.
- **Decision (flag for Phase 6):** if calibrated PDs average about 8%, the provisional
  0–20% "Very Low" band will contain most borrowers. We'll measure the actual band distribution
  after calibration before changing anything.

## 3. Keys and join plan

| Table | Joins to | Observed coverage of training applicants | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| bureau | SK_ID_CURR | 85.7% | median 4 credits per applicant, max 116 |
| bureau_balance | SK_ID_BUREAU → bureau | 45.1% of bureau credits have monthly rows | 43,041 SK_ID_BUREAU values have no bureau row |
| previous_application | SK_ID_CURR | 94.6% | median 4 previous applications |
| POS_CASH_balance | SK_ID_CURR (and SK_ID_PREV) | 94.1% | 37,422 SK_ID_PREV not in previous_application |
| installments_payments | SK_ID_CURR (and SK_ID_PREV) | 94.8% | 38,847 SK_ID_PREV not in previous_application |
| credit_card_balance | SK_ID_CURR (and SK_ID_PREV) | 28.3% | 11,372 SK_ID_PREV not in previous_application |

**Decisions:**
1. Every side table is **aggregated to one row per SK_ID_CURR before joining**, so joins are
   always one-to-one and can't multiply rows. `bureau_balance` is aggregated first to
   SK_ID_BUREAU, then to SK_ID_CURR through `bureau`.
2. POS, instalment and card tables are aggregated **directly on their own SK_ID_CURR**, not
   through `previous_application`. That way records whose SK_ID_PREV is missing from
   `previous_application` are still used. They are all dated before the application, so this is safe.
3. 43,041 `bureau_balance` credits that can't be linked to an applicant are dropped, and the
   number dropped is logged.
4. After the join, "no records" is a real signal (e.g. no bureau history), so each source gets a
   `HAS_<TABLE>` indicator. Its missing aggregates are left missing and are not filled with 0.

## 4. Temporal fields and leakage checks

**Observed:** all history columns end at or before day 0 / month 0 (`DAYS_INSTALMENT` and
`DAYS_ENTRY_PAYMENT` max −1; all `MONTHS_BALANCE` max ≤ 0). There is no evidence that the
current loan's own repayments appear in the side tables.

| Column | Positive values | Interpretation | Decision |
|---|---:|---|---|
| bureau.DAYS_CREDIT_ENDDATE | 602,603 | Planned remaining duration, known at application | Keep |
| previous_application.DAYS_LAST_DUE_1ST_VERSION | 224,392 | Originally planned last due date, known at application | Keep |
| bureau.DAYS_CREDIT_UPDATE | 17 | Defined as "days *before* application", so a positive value contradicts the definition | Set to missing |

**Observed placeholders (365243):** `DAYS_EMPLOYED` in train (55,374) and test (9,274), and five
`previous_application` DAYS columns (40,645 to 934,444 rows each). **Decision:** replace with
missing and add a flag. Left as is, the placeholder would distort means, ratios and the linear model.

**Other leakage risks and how they're handled:**
- `TARGET` and `SK_ID_CURR` are never features. A test will enforce this.
- Every fitted step (imputation, encoding, scaling, winsorising, any resampling) is fitted on the
  training split only, inside an sklearn `Pipeline`.
- Side-table aggregations don't use `TARGET`, so building them on all applicants (train + test)
  doesn't leak labels. Target encoding is not used.
- The calibration split is used only to fit the calibrator. The test split is used once, at the end.

## 5. Data quality

- **Observed:** of the 55,374 `DAYS_EMPLOYED` placeholder rows, 99.96% are pensioners
  (55,352) and 22 are unemployed. Their TARGET rate is 5.40%, against 8.66% for everyone else. The
  placeholder carries information, which is why we keep a flag for it rather than only blanking it.
- **Observed:** `AMT_INCOME_TOTAL` has a median of 147,150, a 99th percentile of 472,500 and a
  maximum of 117,000,000. **Decision:** tree models use the raw values. For logistic regression, take the
  log, then clip at percentiles estimated on the training split.
- **Observed:** `CODE_GENDER` has 4 rows with `XNA`, and `NAME_FAMILY_STATUS` has 2 rows with
  `Unknown`. **Decision:** treat both as missing.
- **Observed:** `ORGANIZATION_TYPE` = `XNA` for 18.0% of rows (the same proportion as pensioners;
  to confirm in Phase 3). `OCCUPATION_TYPE` is missing for 96,391 rows (31.3%).
- **Observed:** 41 of the 122 training columns are more than 50% missing, mostly building
  descriptors that come as `_AVG/_MEDI/_MODE` triples. **Decision (Phase 3):** test whether one of each
  triple is enough, rather than keeping all three.
- **Observed:** 99.92% of bureau credits are in "currency 1", and 1,408 rows are in currencies 2–4,
  whose exchange rates are unknown. **Decision:** exclude non-currency-1 rows from bureau *amount*
  sums, but still count them.
- **Observed:** no duplicate applicant rows (0, ignoring the ID), and train and test share no IDs.
- **Limitation:** the currency of the application amounts is not stated. All amounts are in the
  dataset's own units, and the dashboard will say so.

## 7. Split plan (confirmed)

**Decision:** stratified random split of `application_train` on `TARGET`, seed 42:
60% train / 15% validation / 10% calibration / 15% test. Each split will have about 8.07% positives
(to be verified when the split is built). **Limitation:** the split is random, not by time, so
performance on future applicants may be lower than measured. `application_test` has no labels.
It is used only for scoring the portfolio and for drift comparison.

## 8. Which components the data can support (updated from Phase 0)

| Component | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Prediction, calibration, SHAP, counterfactuals | ✅ Supported | Labelled, unique applicants. Actionable amounts are present and never missing (AMT_CREDIT 0 missing, AMT_ANNUITY 12 missing). |
| Survival on `TARGET` | ❌ Not supported | No event time. "First Y installments" with Y undisclosed. No censoring information. |
| **Survival, alternative** | ✅ Optional, scoped | `installments_payments` has scheduled and actual payment days for previous loans. Time from first instalment to first payment more than k days late, censored at the last observed instalment. Population: *previous Home Credit loans*, not current applicants. |
| Rating / delinquency migration | ✅ Supported, scoped | `bureau_balance` has monthly status (0, 1–5 DPD buckets, C closed, X unknown). 811,206 bureau credits have 2 or more months. Observed transitions for *bureau credits at other lenders*. |
| Early warning, static | ✅ Supported | Calibrated PD thresholds, plus pre-application red flags (recent DPD, refusals, high utilisation). |
| Early warning, deterioration of the current loan | ⚠️ Simulation only | No snapshots after the application. |
| Expected loss | ⚠️ Assumption-based | PD from the model. EAD proxy: AMT_CREDIT (45,000 to 4,050,000, never missing). No observed LGD. |
| Monte Carlo / stress | ⚠️ Assumption-based | Correlation ρ and LGD assumed. Stress tests are feature-perturbation scenarios. |
| Drift | ⚠️ Offline only | Train (307,511) vs application_test (48,744). No dates, no labels for test. |
| Fairness | ✅ With caveats | CODE_GENDER (F 202,448 / M 105,059), age bands (all 7,876 or more rows), family status. The observed base rates differ by group (e.g. M 10.1% vs F 7.0%). These are differences in the data, not evidence of model bias. |

## 9. Research questions, revised

- RQ1–RQ4 and RQ6 stand as written.
- **RQ5 revised:** *What temporal risk patterns can be estimated from applicants' pre-application
  credit histories (delinquency-state migration in bureau records; time to first late payment on
  previous loans), and how do those patterns relate to the TARGET outcome?* The original
  time-to-default question for the current loan can't be answered with this data.
